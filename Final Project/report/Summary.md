# Financial Sales and Profitability Analysis

## Objective
The objective of this project was to analyze sales, profitability, customer segments, countries, products, and discounting patterns using Python and Pandas. The analysis identifies key profit drivers, efficient business areas, and loss-making combinations that need corrective action.

## Dataset
The dataset contains 700 records and 12 fields covering Segment, Country, Product, Discount Band, Units Sold, Manufacturing Price, Sale Price, Gross Sales, Discounts, Net Sales, COGS, and Profit.

## Key Findings
- Government was the highest-profit customer segment, generating approximately ₹11.39M in profit.
- Channel Partners was the most efficient segment, with a profit margin of 73.13%.
- Enterprise was loss-making, with approximately ₹0.61M in losses and a negative profit margin of -3.13%.
- USA generated the highest net sales of ₹25.02M, while France generated the highest total profit of ₹3.70M.
- Germany recorded the highest country-level profit margin at 15.66%.
- Paseo was the top product by both sales (₹33M) and profit (₹4.7M).
- Amarilla had the highest product-level profit margin at 15.86%.
- Medium discounting generated the highest sales, but low discounting produced the highest total profit.
- No-discount transactions achieved the highest profit margin of 21.86%, while high-discount transactions had the lowest margin of 9.07%.
- The Enterprise–Carretera combination recorded an overall loss of about ₹2.2M. Twelve of its 15 transactions, or 80%, were loss-making.

## Recommendations
- Protect and expand the Government–Paseo combination, which generated ₹14.8M in sales and ₹3.0M in profit.
- Review Enterprise–Carretera pricing, discounts, and COGS immediately because most transactions in this combination are loss-making.
- Avoid broad high-discount strategies because high discounts produced the lowest profit margin.
- Use targeted low-to-medium discounts to preserve sales volume while protecting profitability.
- Study the pricing, product mix, and cost practices behind Channel Partners and Amarilla to identify strategies that can improve lower-margin areas.
